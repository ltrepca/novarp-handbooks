Police Handbook & Standard Operating Procedures

LSPD · BCSO · SASP

Everyone: the basics All departments

Three things run every department on Nova: duty, the third eye, and the tablet. Learn these first and the rest of the handbook is detail.

Duty

Nothing works until you clock in. Press F9 (or type /duty) anywhere, use the duty point at your station, or the radial menu's Toggle Duty. Off duty you get no dispatch calls, no officer blips, no MDT roster entry and no department tools — that is deliberate, so clock out when you go civilian.

Third eye

Hold Left Alt and look at a person, vehicle or object. Every department action — cuff, search, revive, stretcher, repair — is an option on the thing you are looking at, and only shows while you are on duty. If an option is missing, you are off duty or too far away.

Tablet

F2 opens the tablet. The Police and Ambulance apps are your MDT: dispatch board, reports, cases, warrants, units, bulletins. Work apps only unlock on duty. Left Alt toggles the cursor while it is open.

Your ID is the PermID

When staff or a form asks for an ID, that means the permanent ID (the # number on the HUD). The only places that still take the short session number are the DOJ inmate tools and /jail — those say so.

Voice is proximity based with three ranges (whisper / normal / shout — cycle with the voice range key; the current range shows on the HUD). Use a radio item for department comms: use it from your inventory (`), join a channel, then hold the radio push-to-talk key. Channels 1–10 are police, EMS should sit on one agreed channel; see codes & channels.

Dispatch & the tablet PD + EMS

Calls arrive as a popup on the right of the screen and on the tablet's dispatch board. Only on-duty members of the job the call is for receive it. A call stays on the board for its timer (five minutes for a gunshot or a person down) and then expires on its own.

Handling a call

  1. Acknowledge. Press Z on the popup (or Respond on the board). Your unit shows as responding so others can cover elsewhere.
  2. Navigate. Every call carries a location and a map blip. G opens it in the tablet if you need the description again.
  3. Call it on radio. "Unit 2-1 en route to the 10-31 at Lake Vinewood." Say what you see on arrival before you act.
  4. Close it. Dismiss (O) once handled so the board reflects reality. Sergeants and above can delete stale calls.

Shots fired by an on-duty officer never create a call — the system ignores police-caused gunfire on purpose, so do not expect your own warning shots to page backup.

Police: starting a shift LSPD · BCSO · SASP

  1. Clock in. F9, or walk onto the duty stand inside Mission Row (just past the front desk) or the Paleto station. The tablet's unit list and your officer blip go live.
  2. Gear up at the armoury (Mission Row, back office — third eye the counter). Everything is bought with department funds at cost:
    ItemPriceNotes
    Handcuffs$50Required to cuff. Buy one, keep it.
    Spike strip$150Deployable; returned to you on pick-up.
    Nightstick / Flashlight$100 / $200Always allowed on your belt.
    Stun gun$500Registered, serial POL.
    Pistol$500Registered, serial POL. Needs your weapon licence.
    Carbine rifle$1,000Open to every grade. Registered, serial POL.
    Pistol / rifle ammo$5Generic by class — one pistol box fits every pistol.
    Department weapons are logged with a POL serial: if one turns up on a civilian, the MDT weapon register tells you whose it was.
  3. Pull a car. Three LSPD garages: the station garage (inside the Mission Row lot) and two more along the east side of the building. Cruiser, Buffalo and the Police Atlantis are available to every grade; the garage menu lets you pick livery and extras before it spawns. Return the car to the same garage when you clock off — abandoned units get towed at your cost.
  4. Check the board. Open F2 → Police → Dispatch and Bulletins. Read the pinned bulletins: BOLOs, active warrants and the shift's assignments live there.
  5. Radio in. Join the dispatch channel (1) and announce yourself with your callsign. Move to a tac channel (2–10) for a scene.
Other station points (Mission Row): fingerprint scanner in the cells corridor, impound lot at the side entrance, helipad on the roof, evidence lockers are ox stashes at the station, and the paperwork trash by the duty stand for anything you want gone.

Other tools

Tackle — G

Sprint at a fleeing suspect and press G within about two metres, roughly facing them. You dive, they go down for three seconds. Three-second cooldown; only works on foot and only for LEO jobs.

Spike strips

Use the Spike Strip item on foot: you kneel and lay it two metres ahead. Any tyre that crosses it bursts. Walk up and press E to pick it up — you get the item back. Server-wide limit of five deployed at once.

Fines — /bill

/bill 750 Reckless driving bills the nearest person; it appears in their bank's Fines tab and they pay when they can. Max $100,000, must be within 8 m. Use the MDT case with charges for anything that also carries jail time.

Speed radars

Station radars fine automatically: $25 up to 10 over, $50 up to 30 over, $250 up to 80 over, $500 beyond that.

Objects

Cones, barriers, road signs, tents, lights and a table can be placed from the police menu for a scene; remove them with the same menu when you leave.

Impound

Third eye a vehicle for the impound options, or drive it to the lot at the side of Mission Row. Choose a hold time; owners release it from the impound with the fee going to the police fund.

Prison facts you should tell the suspect. Sentences are in real minutes. Inmates can shorten their time by working out, cleaning and kitchen duty inside, and can shop at the commissary. Anyone who digs out (a shovel is involved) triggers a 10-99 to every on-duty unit with a blip on the prison — the breakout is a real pursuit.

Police: standard operating procedures Follow in order

Traffic stop

  1. Call it. Radio the plate, vehicle and location before lights go on. Wait for a second unit on a stop with more than two occupants.
  2. Position. Park offset behind the vehicle, lights on. Approach on the passenger side at night.
  3. Identify. Ask for licence and reason for the stop, then ID Check them through the third eye so the name on the report is right.
  4. Decide. Warning, /bill for an infraction, or arrest for a misdemeanour/felony. Speeding alone is a fine, never a cell.
  5. Clear. "Unit clear, resuming patrol" on radio, dismiss any linked dispatch.

Arrest

  1. Control first. Suspect stops or is tackled → Cuff. Say the reason for the arrest out loud; that line is what the DOJ will ask about later.
  2. Search. Third eye → Search. Seize contraband and unregistered weapons into your inventory or an evidence locker; leave personal items alone unless they are evidence.
  3. Transport. Escort to the car, Put In Vehicle (they go in the back), drive to Mission Row. Read them their rights on the way — the right to a DOJ lawyer is real here.
  4. Book them in the MDT. F2 → Police → Profiles → their name → new Report with the charges. Officer I and up can write reports; Senior Patrol Officer (grade 3) and up create cases and warrants. Attach the seized-item list and a mugshot if you took one.
  5. Sentence. Total the charges. Guideline: infraction 0 min (fine only), misdemeanour 10–30 min, felony 30–90 min, capital 90–240 min. Stack charges but stay under 240 unless a Lieutenant signs off.
  6. Jail. Third eye → Send to Prison, enter the minutes. The suspect keeps their phone and can call a lawyer from inside; DOJ can bail or reduce, so the report must be complete before they go in.

Use of force ladder

Presence → verbal → tackle or stun gun → nightstick → firearm. Move up only when the step below fails or the suspect is armed. Shots at a fleeing unarmed suspect are a staff ticket against you. Never fire from a moving vehicle at another moving vehicle unless being fired upon.

Pursuits

  1. Primary unit calls the pursuit: direction, vehicle, speed, reason. Secondary units call in as they join; no more than three units unless a Sergeant authorises.
  2. Spike ahead, not behind. A unit gets ahead on the radio-called route and lays a strip; every other unit backs off so the suspect drives into it.
  3. PIT only under 60 mph and only when a Sergeant or above is in the pursuit. Air unit (helipad on the roof) takes over calling once it is up.
  4. Terminate when the danger to the public exceeds the offence: dense traffic, oncoming lanes, off-road you cannot follow. Say "terminating" — the tablet warrant does the rest.

Reports and evidence

A report per incident, a case per investigation, a warrant per person you want held. Reports carry the officer, the civilians and the suspects involved so the DOJ can read the whole story. Bulletins are for the department: BOLOs, shift notes, roster changes. Evidence you seize and want kept goes into an evidence stash at the station with a note in the report of which slot.

Codes & channels Reference

Radio channels

  • 1 — police dispatch (all units)
  • 2–10 — police tactical channels (one per scene)
  • EMS — agree one channel at shift start (11 is customary) and announce it to PD dispatch
  • 420 / 421 / 422 — gang channels, stay off them

Codes used on Nova

  • 10-31 gunshot / crime in progress
  • 10-52 person down, EMS needed
  • 10-79 deceased
  • 10-99 prison break
  • Code 4 scene secure, no further units
  • Officer down panic — tablet dispatch to every unit

Sentencing guideline (minutes)

  • Infraction — 0, fine with /bill
  • Misdemeanour — 10 to 30
  • Felony — 30 to 90
  • Capital — 90 to 240 (Lieutenant sign-off above 240)

Money that moves automatically

  • Revive fee — $2,000 patient → medic
  • Self check-in — $2,000 (only under two EMS on duty)
  • Radar fines — $25 / $50 / $250 / $500
  • Impound release — fee → police fund
